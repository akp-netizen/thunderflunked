Northwest Iowa Community College - Faculty Development Game. This file contains
final, clean copy for every physical component. Paste each block into your
existing Canva templates. Brand colors: NCC red #A4343A, NCC gray #676767. Use
good contrast and text labels (not color alone). No emojis. Keep course
presentation completely straight-faced.

**Decks in this file**
1. Course Cards (10 unique; print **two copies each** = 20 cards)
2. Public Role Cards (6)
3. Thunderstruck Card and Thunderflunked Card
4. Thunderflunked Playbook

## 2. Public Role Cards (6)

Front of every role card (shared line): *Your Role is the perspective you are
responsible for bringing into team decisions. It does not give you special
powers. Use it to challenge the group when that part of the course is being
overlooked.*

**Student Whisperer** - Watch the student experience: engagement, belonging, and
who may be quietly struggling. Ask: "Whose experience are we not hearing?"

**Learning Police** - Watch alignment: do the activities, practice, and
assessments actually match the stated learning? Ask: "Does this match what we
said students would learn?"

**Burnout Bouncer** - Watch faculty capacity: is this plan sustainable for a real
human instructor? Ask: "Can one person actually keep doing this?"

**Receipts Department** - Watch the evidence: what does the data actually show
before we decide what it means? Ask: "What do we actually know versus assume?"

**Efficiency Gremlin** - Watch for scale: is there a reusable, lower-effort way to
solve this for everyone? Ask: "Is there a version of this that scales?"

**Professional Buzzkill** - Watch consequences: what does this decision cost us
later that it does not cost us now? Ask: "What does this cost us in three weeks?"

*Roles not drawn at a table are shown on screen as Unrepresented Perspectives.*

## 4. Thunderflunked Playbook (card or half-sheet)

> **Your job is persuasion, not vandalism.** You almost never need to lie. You
> need the team to adopt reasonable-sounding, poor decisions and to feel good
> about it.
>
> **Good moves**
> - Push a reasonable idea too far ("let's contact every single student personally").
> - Favor individualized work when a scalable fix would do.
> - Spend faculty capacity and time on low-priority issues.
> - Encourage over-engineering and elaborate new systems late in the term.
> - Argue for quick fixes that create costs later.
> - Delay a useful action ("let's just monitor and see").
> - Occasionally support a genuinely good idea so you stay unsuspected.
>
> **Avoid** being the obvious villain. If the table can predict your vote, you
> have failed. The danger you create is a team that accepts poor decisions.
>
> **Emergency Sabotage (one time, optional).** Available at Checkpoint 2, 3, or 4;
> it expires after CP4. It is small by design and can never sink the course on its
> own. You are offered it during **your own private turn** at those checkpoints
> (choose Activate or Save For Later) - no separate step, so using it does not
> reveal you.

## 5. Secret Mission Deck (18 cards)

Every player draws one, independent of allegiance. Missions reward intentional behavior. Claim privately on the device during a Mission Check (the private checks at CP1, CP3, and CP5), only when the card says you may and you have met the condition. Completion is a small secondary recognition and does not decide the winner.

Role-specific missions (03, 06, 10, 14, 17): deal missions after Role Cards are drawn, remove any mission that names a Role nobody drew, and a player whose mission names their own Role trades it before play begins.

1. **(Claim at any Mission Check: CP1, CP3, or CP5)** Get another player to support proactive outreach to a student who has not asked for help.
2. **(Claim at any Mission Check: CP1, CP3, or CP5)** Get the team to explicitly discuss the difference between a student problem and a course problem before deciding.
3. **(Claim at CP5 / endgame only)** Get the Efficiency Gremlin to back a reusable, scalable solution at least once.
4. **(Claim at any Mission Check: CP1, CP3, or CP5)** Get the team to look at course-activity or practice data (not just grades) before choosing an action.
5. **(Claim at CP5 / endgame only)** Convince the team to decline adding at least one new thing in the Final Stretch.
6. **(Claim at CP3 or CP5)** Get the Receipts Department to state out loud what the evidence does and does not show.
7. **(Claim at CP5 / endgame only)** Shift another player off a position they argued for earlier. Change at least one mind during the game.
8. **(Claim at any Mission Check: CP1, CP3, or CP5)** Get the team to choose a whole-class response over an individual one at least once.
9. **(Claim at CP5 / endgame only)** Persuade the team to choose an option because it protects Faculty Capacity, and keep Faculty Capacity out of Critical for the entire game.
10. **(Claim at CP3 or CP5)** Get the Burnout Bouncer to raise sustainability before the team finalizes a decision.
11. **(Claim at CP5 / endgame only)** Get the team to fix gradebook accuracy (zeros / blank cells) at some point in the semester.
12. **(Claim at any Mission Check: CP1, CP3, or CP5)** Name a specific Course Reality from your Course Card and get the team to factor it into a decision.
13. **(Claim at CP5 / endgame only)** Get the team to intentionally defer a lower-priority issue rather than address everything.
14. **(Claim at any Mission Check: CP1, CP3, or CP5)** Get the Student Whisperer to speak up for a student who has gone quiet.
15. **(Claim at CP5 / endgame only)** Persuade the team to choose a fix that improves Course Quality over a quicker option, and end the game with Course Quality in the Normal band (60+).
16. **(Claim at CP3 or CP5)** Get the team to connect a performance gap to a practice-to-assessment alignment issue, not just effort.
17. **(Claim at CP5 / endgame only)** Get the Professional Buzzkill to talk the team out of a decision by naming its later cost.
18. **(Claim at CP5 / endgame only)** Be the player who proposes the option the team ultimately adopts at three or more checkpoints.

## 6. Resource & Support Deck (two categories)

Physical reference cards for the real tools and services players cite in discussion.
**Give every card a category label** so faculty learn to tell the two apart:

- **COURSE TOOL** - something you do *inside* the course (Canvas) to manage the
  learning environment, surface concerns, communicate, and assess.
- **STUDENT SUPPORT** - a real NCC *service* you connect a student with when the
  evidence shows the student would benefit from outside help.

Suggested card layout for every card: the **category label** (color-coded: NCC gray
for COURSE TOOL, NCC red for STUDENT SUPPORT, with the text label too - never color
alone), the **name**, then four short lines: **What it is / When it may help / How
faculty use or connect / Limitations.**

The teaching pattern the deck supports:
**Notice the evidence -> decide what *kind* of need it is (support, course design,
communication, advising/persistence, or accessibility) -> respond at the right scale
-> use the right tool and/or support -> reassess.** A support need and a course-design
need call for different cards; most student problems need a course response first.

Verified against official NCC sources (nwicc.edu, Oct 2026). Two NCC-specific facts
below - Brainfuse's 15-hours-per-year allowance and the Panorama Accessibility tool
name - are verified NCC information confirmed for this build.

### 6A. Course Tools (inside Canvas)

> **COURSE TOOL - Dropout Detective**
> **What:** Early-alert system to identify/document a student persistence concern and
> route it to the student's advisor.
> **When:** A student shows a persistence-risk pattern (disengaged, missed work,
> stopped logging in) that advising should know about. It is NOT tutoring.
> **How:** Canvas left navigation -> Dropout Detective -> select the student ->
> **Notes** -> write a note to the advisor -> activate the alert. The note is
> instructor-to-advisor communication, not a student-facing message.
> **Limitations:** Flags, documents, and activates an advisor; it does not teach,
> tutor, or fix the course. One alert per genuinely at-risk student - not a class-wide fix.

> **COURSE TOOL - Canvas Grades / Entering Zeros**
> **What:** The gradebook; entering zeros for missing work instead of blank cells.
> **When:** Early, and at end of term, so grades reflect reality.
> **How:** Gradebook -> enter 0 for missing submissions; record last date of attendance
> where required.
> **Limitations:** Accuracy is not outreach - an accurate zero still needs a message if
> you want the student to act.

> **COURSE TOOL - Message Students Who...**
> **What:** Send a targeted message from the gradebook to a filtered group (e.g.
> students who have not submitted an item).
> **When:** Fast, targeted outreach without emailing the whole class.
> **How:** Gradebook -> assignment column menu -> Message Students Who... -> pick the
> condition.
> **Limitations:** Relies on student follow-through; it is a nudge, not a guarantee.

> **COURSE TOOL - Announcements / Scheduled Announcements**
> **What:** Class announcements, which can be written ahead and scheduled to post.
> **When:** Whole-class communication; spreading load and keeping timing consistent.
> **How:** Announcements -> compose -> optionally set a future post date.
> **Limitations:** Whole-class by design - not the tool for one student's individual issue.

> **COURSE TOOL - Canvas Modules / Start Here**
> **What:** Content organized into modules with requirements/prerequisites and a clear
> Start Here path.
> **When:** Creating a sensible order through content and onboarding late-adds.
> **How:** Modules -> build a Start Here module; set requirements/prerequisites.
> **Limitations:** Helps students who engage; does not reach a student who has stopped
> showing up.

> **COURSE TOOL - Rubrics**
> **What:** A criteria-based rubric attached to an assignment so expectations and scoring
> are explicit.
> **When:** Students are unclear what "good" looks like, or grading must be consistent and
> defensible.
> **How:** Assignment -> + Rubric -> define criteria and point values.
> **Limitations:** Clarifies expectations; does not close a skill gap on its own.

> **COURSE TOOL - SpeedGrader / Reusable Feedback**
> **What:** Grade in one place and reuse common feedback (comment library) across
> submissions.
> **When:** Many students share the same issue; cutting grading time.
> **How:** SpeedGrader -> comment -> save to / reuse from the comment library.
> **Limitations:** Feedback only helps if students can find it - point them to where it lives.

> **COURSE TOOL - Course Analytics / Access Report**
> **What:** Activity and access data showing who is engaging with the course and content.
> **When:** Investigating whether a problem is engagement vs. a shared design gap.
> **How:** Course Analytics / a student's Access Report in People.
> **Limitations:** Shows what happened, not why - pair it with the student's actual work.

> **COURSE TOOL - Panorama Accessibility**
> **What:** Checks course documents, pages, and media for accessibility (headings, alt
> text, contrast, captions) and helps remediate them.
> **When:** Making sure all students have equitable access to the materials.
> **How:** Open the Panorama accessibility report in the course and work through flagged
> items.
> **Limitations:** Addresses material access; a documented individual need still routes to
> Accessibility / Accommodation Services (a STUDENT SUPPORT).

> **COURSE TOOL - Simple Syllabus**
> **What:** A consistent, up-to-date syllabus of record.
> **When:** Keeping the syllabus and the live course (due dates, schedule) in agreement -
> the CP1 syllabus-vs-Canvas mismatch is exactly this.
> **How:** Maintain the syllabus of record; reconcile Canvas due dates to it.
> **Limitations:** Fixes the course inconsistency, not a student who already acted on the
> wrong date.

> **COURSE TOOL - Grade Publish**
> **What:** Posting/publishing final grades through the approved end-of-term process.
> **When:** The Final Stretch; confirm accuracy first.
> **How:** Follow the approved Grade Publish process at end of term.
> **Limitations:** Final and visible - verify accuracy before publishing.

> **COURSE TOOL - Course Improvement Survey**
> **What:** End-of-term student feedback on the course.
> **When:** The Final Stretch, as part of finishing accurately and improving next term.
> **How:** Deploy the Course Improvement Survey near end of term.
> **Limitations:** Informs future terms, not the current cohort.

### 6B. Student Supports (real NCC services)

> **STUDENT SUPPORT - Learning Center (Student Success Center)**
> **What:** NCC's academic-support center in Building A: free tutoring (drop-in,
> one-to-one, or small group) in most program areas, plus a computer lab, study rooms,
> and testing/proctoring.
> **When:** A student needs academic help with course content. A natural first option for
> on-campus students - and online students may use it too.
> **How:** Refer the student to the Learning Center in Building A; 712-324-5061 /
> learncenter@nwicc.edu. No appointment required for drop-in tutoring.
> **Limitations:** In-person location (Building A); best when a student can get to campus.
> Tutoring supports the student - it does not fix an unclear assignment or a course-design gap.

> **STUDENT SUPPORT - Brainfuse Online Tutoring**
> **What:** Free online tutoring NCC students reach any time through the sidebar link in
> any Canvas course.
> **When:** A student needs academic help and in-person hours or location are a barrier -
> especially accessible for online students, but open to others as well.
> **How:** Click the Brainfuse link in the sidebar of any Canvas course. No appointment needed.
> **Limitations:** NCC students receive **15 hours of Brainfuse tutoring per year** - it is
> not unlimited, and it supplements rather than replaces course instruction.

> **STUDENT SUPPORT - TRiO Student Support Services**
> **What:** A federally funded program offering eligible students academic advising, career
> and personal counseling, financial-aid advising, professional and peer tutoring by
> appointment, transfer assistance, and Lunch & Learn workshops - all free.
> **When:** An ELIGIBLE student with broader persistence/support needs would benefit from
> wraparound support, not just one tutoring session.
> **How:** Encourage an eligible student to apply (TRiO application on NCC's Academic Support
> page). Services are free to those who qualify.
> **Limitations:** **Eligibility required** - a student must be a first-generation college
> student, meet federal income guidelines, OR have a documented disability, and be a U.S.
> citizen/resident. The program serves a limited number of students. **Not every student
> qualifies - do not assume universal access.**

> **STUDENT SUPPORT - Accessibility / Accommodation Services**
> **What:** NCC's coordination of accommodations and support for students with a documented
> disability.
> **When:** The evidence points to an access need (not an effort or content-help need).
> **How:** Direct the student to apply for accommodations (Application for Accommodation for
> Disability plus records-release paperwork) through NCC's accommodation process.
> **Limitations:** Requires documentation and the student's request; accommodations are
> arranged through the process, not granted ad hoc in the course.

> **STUDENT SUPPORT - Academic Advising**
> **What:** The student's assigned advisor, who helps with persistence, course selection,
> staying on track, and connecting to NCC resources. Advising is required each semester.
> **When:** A persistence/path concern bigger than one assignment - and the destination of a
> Dropout Detective alert.
> **How:** Loop in the student's advisor directly or via a Dropout Detective note/alert.
> **Limitations:** Advisors guide persistence and pathway - they are not the content tutor
> for your course.

> **STUDENT SUPPORT - NCC Library**
> **What:** The NCC Library, which supports all of the college's programs with research help
> and resources.
> **When:** A student (or an assignment) needs research, source, or information-literacy help.
> **How:** Point students to the Library's research help and resources.
> **Limitations:** Research/resource support, not general subject tutoring.

**Modality cue for the table (print on the deck divider, not as a rule):** online courses
-> Brainfuse is usually most accessible; on-campus -> the Learning Center (Student Success
Center) is natural; CTE/hands-on -> check whether the real need is practice, clearer
directions, or an accommodation before defaulting to tutoring. The Learning Center serves
online students too, and Brainfuse serves on-campus students too - modality narrows the
*most accessible* option, it does not lock it.

## 7. Faculty Semester Check (takeaway card)

A keepsake players take with them.

> **The five checks**
> 1. First Week - **Look before reacting.** What does the evidence actually show?
> 2. Early Semester - **Build systems that absorb change.** Will this still work
>    when the roster shifts?
> 3. First Assessment - **Investigate before deciding.** Is it effort, design, or
>    both?
> 4. Midterm - **Prioritize and scale.** What can you do well, and what will you
>    deliberately not do?
> 5. Final Stretch - **Finish sustainably and accurately.** What do you protect,
>    repair, and refuse to add?
>
> **The point of the game**
> Good course design is not about knowing who has the bad idea. It is about having
> a process that catches the bad idea.

## 8. Table Instruction / Reference Card

Keep one per table.

> **Flow:** Join -> register players -> draw Role Cards + Secret Missions -> draw
> Course Card -> trade (physical, optional) -> register course -> deal 15 -> draft
> 10 (5 become Rejected) -> hand off 5 to the next team, receive 5 -> CP1 -> CP2 ->
> CP3 -> CP4 -> CP5 -> Final Accusation -> Outcome -> Debrief.
>
> **Resources (0-100):** Student Success, Course Quality, Faculty Capacity,
> Time/Resources. Normal 60+, Strained 30-59, Critical below 30.
>
> **Private Player Check** happens after every checkpoint. One device, one player
> at a time; everyone else looks away.
>
> **Win:** The course survives if the Overall Course Score is at least **58** and
> no resource is below **30**. Thunderstruck win if it survives; the one
> Thunderflunked wins if it fails. Catching the Thunderflunked is a bonus, not the
> win.
>
> **You cannot advance yourselves.** The Game Master controls the room; use your
> screen's Refresh button after each change.
